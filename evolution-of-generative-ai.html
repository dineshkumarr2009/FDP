Session One · Setting the tone

The Evolution of
Generative AI

Six acts, three and a half years, and one tweet that nobody thought was important.

By the end of this you will not know more tools. You will know where you are standing.

Verified July 2026

Act 0 · 2017 to mid 2022

Before the tweet

All of it was built in the open. Not one staff room anywhere was talking about it.

Five years of build up. Zero public attention. Then one tweet.

Act 1 · 30 November 2022

The tweet

No press conference. No keynote. No launch video. A blog post and one casual line on X.

today we launched ChatGPT. try talking with it here: chat.openai.com Sam Altman · 30 November 2022

Reports at the time noted that OpenAI's own board learned of the public launch from that post. Within two months it had 100 million users, the fastest adoption of any consumer product ever recorded.

The largest technology shift of our working lives arrived as a tweet, and it did not ask anyone's permission.

One year on: how a text only launch took over the internet Bloomberg Technology · ChatGPT's One Year Anniversary
Play 0:00 to 0:40 only. Stop it the moment the point lands. This clip exists to make the room feel how small the moment was, not to inform them.

Act 2 · 2023

The race, all in one year

Twelve months to go from one product to an industry. Tap any line to open it.

OpenAI DevDay 2023 · opening keynote OpenAI · the first stage launch of the AI era
Show 26:28 to 27:30 and nothing else. That sixty seconds is a person building software by describing it out loud. Every teacher in the room understands what it implies without you saying a word. If you are short on time, this is the only clip in the whole session you cannot cut.

Act 3 · 2024

It stops being a text box

The year AI stopped writing paragraphs and started making things you could listen to and watch.

Two AI hosts discussing a document nobody wrote a script for Google · NotebookLM audio discussions
Forty seconds, then stop. Do not introduce it. Do not name the tool. Let it play, stop it, then say: no human recorded that, and it came from a PDF. The silence in the room after this is the point of the whole segment. Better still, replace this with your own. Run one of your units through NotebookLM and record forty seconds. Your subject in your students' ears beats Google's marketing every time.

This is the exact moment the session you are sitting in became possible.

Act 4 · Dec 2024 to early 2025

The floor falls out

DeepSeek arrives from Hangzhou with open weights, a published paper and a fraction of the cost. Global markets move on it inside a week.

No launch event. No keynote. No press tour. A model release on GitHub and a technical paper, exactly the way this whole story started three years earlier.

The assumption that this stays expensive, closed and American broke in about a week.

For this room that is not a footnote. It is the act that says the game is open, the cost of entry is falling, and the next capable model may not come from where you expect. Say it plainly and let it sit.

Act 5 · 2025 to now

From talking to doing

Reasoning models that think before answering. Agents that work for hours unattended. Tools that write and run their own code.

NotebookLM grows a Studio: video overviews, mind maps, slide decks, infographics, quizzes, flashcards. Google begins folding it into Gemini. New frontier models now land every few weeks, and the tracking sites refresh hourly to keep up.

Nobody is winning. Every model is good. The question stopped being which AI is best and became which AI for which task.

You are not late. You are early, and you are in the region that is ahead.

For thirty years we waited for the West to adopt something first. On this one, Asia is at ninety two and North America has dropped to sixty seven. This is not a catching up session. It is a staying ahead session.

The gap is not technology. It is the twenty nine percent. Eighty eight out of every hundred of your students are already using this. Twenty nine of them think you can guide them on it. Closing that is not a software problem. It is a teacher training problem, and that is why we are in this room.
