Fill in the six blanks

Do not think about prompting. Think about the thing you actually have to do this week. Answer honestly, in your own words, and the prompt writes itself.

Copy this, then paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT

      

    

    
Whatever comes back, do not judge it yet. Say one of these to it: shorter. simpler English. more Indian examples. try again. The first output is a draft, never an answer. That second message is where the real skill lives.

Fix a weak prompt

    
Easier than starting from blank. Here is one that fails. Repair it, then check your repair.


    

      
The weak promptI need to make my teaching interesting

      
Your turn
Rewrite it using the six blanks above. Who are you. Who are they. What exactly. What is hard about it. What does done look like. What must not happen.

    


    
Then paste your repair here. This checks it against the six and tells you what is still missing. It is a checklist, not a grader, so use your own judgement on top of it.


    

      

        

          
          
        

        
      

      

        

        
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    03
    
    
    Why this works
    
    
    A circular lands in your inbox: "Do the needful for the seminar." The Assistant Professor books a hall. The administrator prints letters. The lab assistant sets up a projector. All three are wrong, and none of them is at fault. The instruction carried no role, no scope, no constraint, no deadline.
    
    
    A weak prompt is a weak circular. You have spent your career decoding these. You are not learning a new skill today. You are pointing an old one at a new place.
    

    
    
      
    In your worldIn a prompt
    
      
    Who is doing itRole
    
      
    For whomAudience
    
      
    Which unitScope
    
      
    Why it is hardThe real problem
    
      
    How long, what formConstraints
    
      
    "Send me a draft first"Iteration
    
    
    

    
    
      The line that stops it interrogating you.
    
      Most tools stall by asking you to clarify. End every prompt with: do not ask me questions, make a sensible assumption, state it in one line at the top, then write the full answer.
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    04
    
    
    Corrected work
    
    
    Read these like an answer script. Struck out is what most of us type. Below it, the same request done properly.
    

    
    
      
    As submittedExplain the 4Ps of marketing
    
      
    As corrected
    You are a commerce lecturer in Bengaluru teaching first-year B.Com students, semester 2. English is their second language and they have no prior marketing exposure. The problem: they can recite the four Ps but cannot apply them to anything. Write a 400-word explainer on Unit 2 built entirely around one Indian brand they use daily. Explain any technical term in brackets the first time it appears. Do not ask me questions; state any assumption at the top and write it.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    As submittedMake a question paper on organisational behaviour
    
      
    As corrected
    Draft an internal assessment for 20 marks, 45 minutes, on motivation theories, for third-semester BBA students. Last time they memorised the theories and failed the application question, so weight it towards application. Five one-mark recall, two five-mark application using an Indian workplace scenario, one five-mark analyse-level. Marking scheme in a separate section. Nothing outside Maslow, Herzberg and McClelland.
    
    
    

    
    
      
    As submittedWrite a mail to parents about attendance
    
      
    As corrected
    Write a 150-word email from a class mentor to the parent of a second-year student whose attendance has fallen to 62 percent. The student is not defiant, something has changed at home and I do not want to accuse anyone. Concerned, not accusatory. Mention the 75 percent requirement once. Invite them to reply with a convenient time. Do not threaten. Indian English, formal but warm.

    Session plan with a hook they've watched
    

    Build a 50-minute session plan on topic for programme
and semester. What is hard: the real reason they switch off.
Open with a hook from a film, advertisement or Indian company
these students already know. Then theory in three moves. Then
one debate question with no correct answer. Close with a
two-minute written reflection. Give timings for each block.
Do not ask me questions; state any assumption at the top.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Case study built to order
    

    Write a 500-word case set in an Indian sector business that
forces students to apply concept. Give the protagonist a real
decision with a genuine trade-off and no obvious answer. End
with four discussion questions rising from understand to
evaluate. Do not state the moral.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Assessment AI cannot do for them
    

    Here is my current assignment. Redesign it so a student using
AI alone cannot complete it, without banning AI. Use local data
collection, in-class defence, personal reflection or process
evidence. Same learning outcome, same marks. Give me two
options.

[paste your assignment]
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Minutes from rough notes
    

    Convert these rough notes into formal minutes for a department
meeting. Attendees, agenda items, decisions taken, action
owners with deadlines, items deferred. Under one page. Neutral
institutional tone. Invent nothing that is not in my notes.

[paste]
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Accreditation narrative
    

    Write a 300-word narrative for our accreditation file on
activity. Third person, past tense. Lead with the outcome,
not the description. Include participation numbers and the
criterion it maps to. No promotional language.

[paste raw details]
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Difficult reply, three versions
    

    Give me three replies to the message below: one firm, one
collaborative, one that buys time. Under 120 words each. Tell
me in one line what each version risks. I am a designation
replying to a designation.

[paste]
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    
        
    Literature gap, from papers you supply
    

    I am attaching number papers on topic. Using only these
sources, build a table: author and year, theory, method, sample,
key finding, stated limitation. Then in 150 words name the gap
they all leave open. Cite the source for every claim. If
something is not in these papers, say so instead of filling
it in.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Reviewer 2, in advance
    

    Act as a hostile Reviewer 2 for a Scopus-indexed journal in
field. Read my draft below. List the six objections you would
raise, hardest first, naming the section each attacks. Do not
compliment anything. Then tell me which two I can actually fix
before submission.

[paste]
    
      
    
      
    
        Closed book, open book.
    
        A general chatbot writes a closed-book exam: confident, fluent, and it will invent a page number the way a bright student does. NotebookLM writes an open-book exam where the book is chained to the desk and every sentence must show its source. The industry calls this RAG.

    09
    
    
    Before you upload anything
    
    
      
      
    • No student records, no personal data, no unpublished institutional documents into any tool you have not checked.
      • 
      
    • No confidential question papers. Ever.
      • 
      
    • Free tools are free for a reason. Know where the data lands before you paste.
      • 
      
    • Verify every fact and every citation before it reaches a student.
      • 
      
    • Declare your AI use, and require your students to declare theirs.
      • 
      
    • Your institution's policy overrides everything on this page.
      • 
    
    
    
    One more, and it matters most. You cannot reliably tell AI writing from human writing, and neither can detection software. Do not punish a student on suspicion. Redesign the task instead. There is a prompt for that in section 05.
