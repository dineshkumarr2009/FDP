NotebookLManswers only from your document, and shows you where every answer came from

Step one · load it and question it

Upload, then interrogate

Open notebooklm.google.com and sign in. On an office account that blocks it, use a personal Gmail.

Click New notebook, then Add sources, and upload the source pack PDF.

In the chat, ask it: what does this say about why students switch off in the first ten minutes?

Tap the little citation number in its answer. Watch it jump to the exact line in the document. It cannot invent, because it only reads what you gave it.

Step two · the podcast

Turn the document into a conversation

In the Studio panel, tap Audio Overview.

Before generating, tap Customise and paste the steer below.

Generate it. It takes a few minutes, so start it and read on.

When it plays, tap Join and ask the hosts a question out loud. They answer from your document, then carry on.

Paste into Customise · the podcast
Explain this to college faculty in simple, warm English.
Focus on the practical shift: what teachers should actually
do differently on Monday for students who learn this way.
Use Indian classroom examples. Assume the listener is a busy
lecturer who has not read the document. Keep it under 8
minutes. Do not just summarise, have a real back-and-forth
where one host pushes the other for specifics.

Free accounts get three audio overviews a day. If yours will not generate, you have used them up. Pair with a neighbour and share one.

Step three · your hands-on

Make your own one-page poster

The challenge

Same document, forty of you, and in seconds you will each have a different one-page poster. Make yours, screenshot it, drop it in the group. First five in get a shout-out.

In Studio, tap Infographic.

Paste the steer below. Notice the last two lines, they are what make it look good instead of plain.

Screenshot the result and share it. Print-ready for a staff-room wall.

Studio · Infographic · your poster
Make a one-page infographic from this document for college
faculty. Title it clearly. Show at most six ideas, each with
a short heading and one line. Focus on what teachers should
actually do differently for students who learn this way.

Make it visually appealing: a clear visual hierarchy, a small
simple icon beside each of the six ideas, generous spacing,
and one accent colour used consistently. Readable at a glance
and still clean when printed in black and white.

Taking too long, or came back plain? Tap Mind Map instead. It is instant every time and still something you can hold up. The point is that you make something, not that everyone makes the identical thing.

G
Geminiwhere the same idea becomes a decorated, illustrated image, and an animated storybook

NotebookLM gives you clean and accurate. Gemini gives you beautiful. Same idea, two tools, and knowing which does which is the real skill. This is called chaining: grounded content from one tool, rich visuals from another.

Step four · the decorated infographic

The same poster, but illustrated

Open gemini.google.com.

In the tools menu, turn on Create images (Nano Banana). If you have it, choose the Pro or Thinking image model for the best text.

Paste one of the six ideas from your NotebookLM poster, then paste the prompt below.

Check every word and figure before you would ever use it with students. Image tools can spell wrong or get a fact wrong.

Gemini · decorated infographic
Create a visually rich, illustrated infographic for college
teachers on how to teach a generation that learns differently.
Show six ideas, each with a hand-drawn style icon, a short
bold heading and one line of text. Warm, friendly, modern
design. Use a maroon and mustard-gold colour scheme on a
cream background. Clear layout, legible text, poster style,
suitable to print and pin on a staff-room wall.

Step five · the animated storybook

Turn a concept into a picture-book

Still in Gemini, start a new prompt.

Paste the storybook prompt below.

Gemini builds an illustrated storybook, page by page, that reads itself aloud. This is the one to make for younger students, or to explain a dry concept gently.

Gemini · animated storybook
Make an animated storybook that tells the story of one teacher
who changes how they teach for a generation that learns
differently. Give it a beginning, a struggle and a hopeful
ending. One running character an Indian teacher would
recognise. Warm children's-book illustration style, soft
colours, one clear picture per page, gentle and human.

Building a big concept? Ask Gemini for a full illustrated explainer of any topic you teach, with diagrams and labelled pictures. The same tool that made the storybook will visualise an entire chapter as one decorated page.

Step six · give it to your students

Turn your notebook into a tutor

Back in NotebookLM, tap Share and send the notebook link to your class. It becomes a tutor they can question at eleven at night, that only ever answers from what you taught them. For most of us this is the one to use on Monday.

Before any of this reaches a student
  • Read it yourself. Verify every fact, figure and citation.
  • Never upload student records, personal data or confidential papers.
  • Tell students when work is AI-assisted, and ask them to do the same.
  • Your institution's policy comes before anything on this page.
